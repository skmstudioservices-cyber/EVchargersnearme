What DC fast charging means

AC chargers feed your car's onboard charger; DC fast chargers feed the battery directly, which is why they are much quicker. Rapid charging slows down above 80% to protect the battery — plan your stop around 80%, not 100%.

Where DC fast chargers are located

  • Highways: every 200–250 km on major corridors — see our highway charging guide.
  • City hubs: malls, metro stations, petrol pumps and dedicated charging bays.
  • Operators: Statiq, ChargeZone, Tata Power and Jio-bp Pulse run most Indian DC units.

What DC fast charging costs

Public charging is billed per kWh. DC fast charging costs more than AC — typically in the range of ₹18–28 per kWh depending on network, state and time of day. Home charging remains the cheapest option for daily use.

❓ DC fast charging stations near me — FAQ

How fast is a DC fast charger?

Most Indian DC units are 25–60 kW and charge a typical EV to 80% in 45–60 minutes. Newer 120–240 kW units on highways are even quicker.

Is CCS2 the same as fast charging?

CCS2 is the connector standard most Indian DC fast chargers use. “CCS2” on a charger almost always means DC fast charging.

Does DC fast charging damage the battery?

Occasional DC fast charging is fine — cars manage battery protection themselves. Charging to 80% and not relying on DC every day keeps the battery healthiest.

How do I find a working fast charger near me right now?

Use our live map to locate CCS2 pins, then check the operator's app for live status before driving there.

Why does charging slow down after 80%?

The car's battery management system reduces charging speed above 80% to protect cell life. That last 20% can take as long as the first 80%.
